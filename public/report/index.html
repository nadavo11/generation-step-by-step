Generation, Step by Step

An image-space audit of streaming and coarse-to-fine generation. We sampled all 17 public DCTdiff and FAR checkpoints, and added one ImageNet-256 checkpoint each for D-AR, FlexVAR, VAR and DetailFlow. NFIG is included in the comparison table; its public repository has no pretrained weights.

What the trajectories show

  1. DCTdiffDCTdiff goes coarse-to-fine in time, without being told to. Across all 14 checkpoints the x̂₀ prediction settles the coarsest band at 28–40% of the 100 network calls and the finest at 70–95%, always in band order. Nothing in the model enforces this. It comes from the noise schedule acting on DCT coefficients whose energy falls with frequency.
  2. DCTdiffModel size changes the result, not the schedule. On FFHQ-128 the settle points of small, mid and mid-deep agree to within 0.02 (coarsest 0.32–0.33, finest 0.72), while FID goes 6.25 → 5.13 → 4.98. Dataset and solver set the schedule instead: ImageNet-64 on the Euler ODE settles latest (0.40 → 0.95), and FFHQ, AFHQ and CelebA on DPM-Solver settle earliest (finest band by 0.71–0.76).
  3. DCTdiffWhere it is weak. At 32² and 64² object identity is often unclear. High-resolution samples look smooth because the representation drops high DCT coefficients by design: FFHQ-512 and AFHQ-512 keep 18 of 64 per 8×8 block, and FFHQ-256 keeps 8 of 16 per 4×4. At 256² it trails latent UViT (FID 5.08 vs 4.26), though it beats it at 512² (7.07 vs 10.89). The ImageNet-64 checkpoints cannot be steered to a named class (see the caveats).
  4. FAREach FAR stage redraws the whole image. The stage predictions are full images, but only the coarsest pixel band converges stage by stage (FAR-H residual 0.51 at stage 2, 0.14 by stage 4). Bands above 1/8 Nyquist stay at 0.7–1.9 until stage 10, so every stage invents new detail that the next one throws away. Stage-1 predictions are patchy mosaics.
  5. FARThe prefix FAR carries forward is never close to a partial image. The context passed between stages decodes to a blur with VAE grid ringing. Even at stage 9, with 10×10 of the 16×16 latent kept, its 1/16–1/4 bands are only about 20% of the way to the final image (residual 0.74–0.84), and its finest bands carry 1.3–1.6× wrong energy. The frequency progression lives in VAE-latent space. In pixels, the last stage makes most of the mid- and high-frequency decisions in a single step.
  6. FARScale makes FAR settle earlier and closer. The stage-9 prediction’s RMSE to the final image drops from 0.123 (B) to 0.094 (L) and 0.082 (H). The coarsest band settles by stage 5 (L), 6 (H) and 7 (B). This tracks the published FID of 4.26 → 3.45 → 3.21.
  7. Net for us: DCTdiff shows that pixel-frequency ordering appears on its own under DCT-space diffusion, but it caps detail by truncating coefficients. FAR’s frequency AR has no pixel-space prefix that resembles the final image until its last stage, so its stages are not a usable coarse-to-fine image sequence.

When each frequency band settles

For each intermediate image we take the luma FFT of its difference from that trajectory's own final sample, split it into octave bands of the Nyquist radius, and divide by the final image's energy in the same band (median over 8 samples). At 0 the band already matches the final image. At 1 the band is either missing or wrong by its full energy. Above 1 it carries extra energy (noise, ringing, or details that will be redrawn). The x-axis is the fraction of sampling work done: network calls for DCTdiff, stages for FAR.

Settle point: the first point after which the band's residual stays below 0.1. A dot at the right edge means the band only matches the final image at the very end. CIFAR-10 has no coarsest band at 32² (no non-DC frequencies below 1/16 Nyquist).

How this was run

  • Official repos at the pinned commits, official configs and checkpoints from Hugging Face (forever208/DCTdiff, figereatfish/FAR). Sampling functions are unchanged. FAR uses a line-for-line copy of sample_tokens_nomask that also stores each stage's latents. DCTdiff wraps ScoreModel.noise_pred to log every call. fp16 autocast is used for both, as in the official evals.
  • DCTdiff x̂₀ = x_t/√ᾱ(t) − √(1−ᾱ)/√ᾱ · ε̂, which is the repo's own ScoreModel.x0_pred. All states are decoded with the official DCT_to_RGB (clip to [−2, 2], zero-fill the truncated coefficients, ×η, block IDCT, 4:2:0 YCbCr → RGB).
  • ffhq128_uvit_mid_4by4.py says depth 18, but the released FFHQ-128 mid checkpoint has depth 16 (130M, the paper's “mid”). We override depth to 16 for that checkpoint only.
  • CelebA ships two checkpoints. Per the config comment, the DPM-Solver one runs at SNR scale 1 with DPM-Solver and the ddim one runs the config's Euler ODE at SNR scale 4.
  • Rows use label indices 207, 360, 387, 974, 88, 979, 417, 279. DCTdiff builds its ImageNet-64 label map from an unsorted os.listdir of the authors' training folder, so these are not the standard ImageNet classes and the mapping was not released. Each column shares one label.
  • The settle metric compares each state with that trajectory's own final sample, not with real data. It measures when the image is decided, not how good it is. Values are medians over 8 samples. FAR's context latents are off the VAE's training distribution, so part of their ringing comes from the decoder: it shows what the state looks like as an image, not what the transformer reads.
  • FID is not re-measured here. The table quotes each paper for the same checkpoint and sampler. All runs used one RTX A5000 each.
